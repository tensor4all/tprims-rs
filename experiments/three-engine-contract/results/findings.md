### What reproduces from the original figure, and what does not

- **The small-case ranking has changed sign.** The figure had tensorcontract
  beating tenferro by 2x to 5x on the small batched GEMMs (`ikb,knb->inb` at
  n = 2, 4, 8, batch 16 and 64) and by 34.6x on the chi = 4 MPS chain. Here
  tenferro is *ahead* on those batched cases (`tf_exec` 0.27 to 0.70, and
  `tf_call` 0.47 to 3.28 depending on the boundary) and the chi = 4 gap is 3.5x
  (`tf_call` / `tc_call` = 14.14 / 4.03). The tenferro revision measured here is
  months newer than the one the figure was taken with, so the figure is not
  reproducible in magnitude or, on those cases, in direction.
- **The large cases reproduce.** `n16` at batch 256 keeps the figure's direction
  (tenferro ahead: 0.82 prebuilt, 0.88 per call), and the c64 MPS chain at
  chi = 32 and 64 is where tensorcontract's own execution is at its best
  (`tp_exec` 1.02 and 1.17).
- **tprims is ahead of tensorcontract's prebuilt execution on every case except
  the two largest MPS ones** (chi = 32 at 1.02, chi = 64 at 1.17, i.e. 2% and 17%
  behind). This is the table issue #63 was written from, and the numbers agree
  with it to two decimal places; `FaerLimit` bounds faer by GEMM volume so that
  the chi = 64 case takes the packed driver instead.

### Reading

- **The small cases are decided by this table, not by the kernels.** At
  chi = 4 the kernel is 0.18 us per step (`tp_exec` / 64) while the host work in
  `tp_call` is 1.3 us, seven times as much; tenferro pays 4.2 us and
  tensorcontract 1.0 us. That is why the whole small end of the ratio table
  looks like a per-call comparison.
- **tenferro pays three to four times what the other two pay per call**, at
  every chi, and roughly chi-independent (4.2-5.0 us per step against 1.0-2.3).
  A cost that does not move with the shape is planning or construction, not
  layout work; the plan of #62 (cpueinsum above `tprims-contract`, taking over
  tenferro's non-BLAS path so that the call path is `Problem` + `Plan`, no N-ary
  tree and no string parsing) is aimed exactly at this row.
- **tprims' 1.3 us per step at chi = 4 is planning**, and the same quantity is
  what `unary-add` measured for the unary update
  (`../unary-add/results/manifest.txt`): 3-16 us per call when a plan is built
  per call and 0.9-3.6 us on a prebuilt plan. Both point the same way, to a plan
  cache or a cheaper lowering, before a per-subblock caller is worth shipping.

## What this does not cover

- **One thread.** The issue asked for 1T and 4T. This record is 1T, and the
  crossover question that needs both widths is measured separately by
  `experiments/faer-limit-sweep/` on the same corpus shapes, at 1T and 4T on this
  host.
- **One host, one rustc, one revision per engine**, and one 3-session A/A repeat
  of a single host state. Nothing here is a cross-host comparison.
- **The corpus is a reconstruction.** Case definitions are the working
  assumptions in `corpus()` in `src/main.rs`, not definitions confirmed against
  the unpublished script the original figure came from.
- **The original figure's numbers are not reproduced.** They cannot be: the
  script is not public and the tenferro revision has moved. What is reproduced is
  the tprims-vs-tensorcontract table of issue #63, and the direction of the large
  cases.
